      Venue A and venue B are constant-product pools. The fee on a swap stays out of the reserves, so each pool’s k is unchanged by the trade. The L1 transaction reverts unless every leg fills and the desk is not poorer. In the split world the opening borrow and buy are their own transaction. Slots in between move the fair price and let other traders push each venue toward it. The sell on B uses the limit from the original quote. Margin can cover a small miss. If it cannot, the loan is still open and the desk holds whatever the failed leg left.

      Each competing trader takes about 1.2% of one pool. A bridge of zero slots, with the sell still inside the same moment, matches the L1 result.

      From toly’s post: Solana’s aim is one global atomic state machine, as fast as the laws of physics allow. An L2, a side chain, or a validium is an external execution environment, and it cannot promise atomic composition with the rest of L1 state. Nothing here contacts a cluster.
