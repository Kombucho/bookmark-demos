Desk

A swap moves the reserves by the amount after the fee, so k does not change and the fee waits in its own bucket. Compound values those fees at the spot price, keeps 10%, and adds the rest in the pool’s ratio. The price stays put and k grows, which is the per-share backing. The mark dips by the cut, then the larger pool earns fees on fees.

The hold is the starting tokens, marked at the new price. The uncompounded LP sees the same price path and the same round trips, and never folds fees in. Minting and redeeming scale both books together, so one share’s mark does not jump.

From @OxSimpleFarmer’s post on Pair Tokens: an ERC-20 share of a full-range Uniswap v4 pool, ETH and NVDA fused into ETHNVDA, with a compound function anyone can call. In that setup the 10% cut buys back another token. This page keeps the cut and does not trade it. Nothing here contacts a chain.
