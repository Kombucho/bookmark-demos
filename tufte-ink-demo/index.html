How the meter counts

The ratio is necessary data ink divided by all the ink in the figure. A mark is data when it is the one encoding of a number or a name: the top of a bar, the stem, the line, the sparkline, the stall name sitting on that line. Redundant ink repeats a number the position already shows: a filled bar body, the digit printed on the bar, a legend swatch. Non-data ink would look the same if the cups changed: the grid, the ground, the border, the 3D sides, the moiré hatch, the legend box, the axis frame.

Redundant ink stays in the denominator and out of the numerator. Erasing it raises the ratio. The cream page is not ink. Blank paper never enters the sum.

The number is the sum of those marks. A stroked mark counts as its length times its stroke width. A filled rectangle or polygon counts as its area. A hatch counts as its strokes, not the bar underneath. A label counts as each character’s share of an em box, 0.45 by 0.7 of the font size squared. The same marks are painted onto a hidden canvas, one color per role, later marks covering earlier ones, and the pixel line is that count. When Pele draws the lines, the pixel sample uses those paths.

Switching from forty-eight stems to one line can ease the ratio: a line is shorter than the stems it replaces. Small multiples add four short axes, so they spend a little non-data ink on purpose. The sparkline sentence is almost all data, and the ratio rises again.

Source

From the skill “Give your AI agents the skill of visualizing data the way Edward Tufte intended” (gnurio/tufte-vdqi-plugin), the post’s rules as a skill for The Visual Display of Quantitative Information. The page applies them in order: erase non-data ink, erase redundant data ink, put the labels on the marks, use a range frame, maximize the data-ink ratio, then small multiples and a sparkline. No passage from the book is reproduced. Sparklines were introduced later, in Beautiful Evidence, and the last step says so.

The thin multi-series line is Mermaid xychart-beta, drawn by Pele 1.7.0 (MIT), vendored as vendor/pele.global.js. The license is vendor/PELE-LICENSE.txt. Pele’s chart always keeps a full frame, tick labels, and a legend gutter, and it will not draw a word-sized sparkline or a range frame that stops at the data. Those layers, the chartjunk, and the small multiples are hand-written SVG. On the thin-line step the range frame is still on, so the page hides Pele’s axis and draws the two lines that stop at 22 and 84 cups.
