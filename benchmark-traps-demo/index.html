Simulated bench

Benchmark traps

Two fictional engines, Lumen and Harbor, share one simulated client. Change a pitfall from the benchmarking notes and the headline names a different winner. Throughput and the latency distribution stay on the page either way.

Every sample is computed in this page. No database is contacted. Lumen and Harbor are not products. The only figures taken from the article are the single-connection sketch, 1000 / (10 + 2.5) = 80 QPS and 1000 / (10 + 15) = 40 QPS, and the note that Geekbench on an r7g.2xlarge is about 15% lower than on an r8g.2xlarge. That 15% is applied here only when a CPU generation is handed to one engine. It is not a query score.

One query, one connection

The article’s sketch: a query of about 10 ms, then the round trip. At 2.5 ms the single connection does 80 queries a second. At 15 ms it does 40. The same client, the same query. The buttons multiply that rate by 1, 10, 50, or 100 connections, the counts the article names, and they leave the server unsaturated. A fixed connection count does not grow to hide a slow round trip.

        Reading Lumen Harbor

        Charts are Mermaid xychart-beta, drawn by Pele 1.7.0 (MIT), vendored as vendor/pele.global.js. The license is vendor/PELE-LICENSE.txt. Pele’s xy chart draws lines and bars. It has no error-bar glyph, so the spread of throughput is the line, the coefficient of variation, and the interquartile range.

        Lumen keeps a fast memory path and a slow disk miss. Harbor is steadier on disk and slower in memory. Those shapes were chosen so the toggles can move the headline. They are not measurements of any database.

        From Ben Dicken’s post and PlanetScale’s “On benchmarking.” The pitfalls in the controls are the ones that article talks through: client and server round trip, matched hardware, RAM versus disk, uniform and Zipfian access, closed versus open loop, coordinated omission, throughput and percentile latency, warmup, checkpoint timing, run-to-run spread, and the list of benchmark crimes. Apples-to-oranges product claims in that article are left off this page.
